# »Alles, was man lesen muss: Die 50 wichtigsten Bücher« (*profil*, 2001)
*(= »Everything You Need to Read: The 50 Most Important Books«)*

This list was published in the Austrian weekly magazine *profil*, issue no. 48 (26 November 2001), pp. 142–149. The jury consisted of seven Austrian writers, booksellers, journalists, and scholars of German literature: Barbara Frischmuth, Wendelin Schmidt-Dengler, Radek Knapp, Ilse Aichinger, Rotraut Schöberl, Michael Köhlmeier, and Barbara Coudenhove-Kalergi.

They were asked to submit a personal ranked list of "50 classics for life". The overall ranking was based on both the number of mentions and the positions assigned to the titles in the individual lists. The jurors could draw on the whole of world literature: poetry (including epic poetry), narrative prose, drama, and essays. The individual lists were available at `www.profil.at/literaturkanon` (see Additional Links below).

We extracted the list from the print edition, transformed it into a table, and enriched it with metadata about authors and works (Author Wikidata ID, Author GND ID, Work Wikidata ID, Work GND ID, Work Goodreads ID). We also added the original languages of the works by reconciling them against Wikidata and normalized historical language varieties to their corresponding standard-language categories (for example, Early Modern Spanish → Spanish). Publication dates were recorded using [EDTF](https://www.loc.gov/standards/datetime/) notation.

## Key Statistics
The ranking comprises 50 positions but 52 individual works because ranks 14 and 41 each include two titles: Goethe's two *Wilhelm Meister* novels and Lewis Carroll's two *Alice* books.

Of the 50 ranked positions, 16 contain works originally written in German, 12 in English, and 8 in French. Together, these 36 positions account for 72% of the list. Using the earliest recorded publication year, 33 of the 50 positions contain works dating from the nineteenth or twentieth century.

William Shakespeare is the only author represented at three different ranks (*Hamlet*, *King Lear*, and *Much Ado About Nothing*). Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, Fyodor Dostoevsky, James Joyce, Franz Kafka, Thomas Mann, Leo Tolstoy, and William Faulkner are each represented at two ranks.

The most striking imbalance concerns gender. Among the 40 identified individual authors – counting Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm separately – 39 are men and only one is a woman: Ingeborg Bachmann.
